# Meta Advertising Policies & Account Health (2025–2026)

Scope: Meta Advertising Standards structure, restricted verticals, special ad categories, rejection/restriction causes, review & appeal flows, and account-health hygiene. Legitimate compliance only. Last researched: 2026-07-22.

Naming note: Meta's policy book is now the **Meta Advertising Standards** (formerly "Advertising Policies"), hosted on the **Meta Transparency Center** (transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/), not the Business Help Center. **Account Quality** is now **Business Support Home** (business.facebook.com/business-support-home), with "Account status overview" inside it; facebook.com/accountquality still resolves. Pre-2024 articles showing "Account Quality" describe the same surface under the old name.

---

## 1. Structure of the Meta Advertising Standards

Current standards (Transparency Center, fetched 2026-07-22), top-level groups:

1. **Unacceptable content** (fully prohibited, no exceptions): Child Sexual Exploitation/Abuse/Nudity; Coordinating Harm and Promoting Crime; Dangerous Organizations and Individuals; Discriminatory Practices; Hateful Conduct; Human Exploitation; Locally Illegal Content/Products/Services; Misinformation (content debunked by third-party fact-checkers); Vaccine Discouragement.
2. **Fraud, Scams and Deceptive Practices**: Fraud/Scams/Deceptive Practices; Unacceptable Business Practices (deceptive or misleading schemes, scams for money or personal info). Meta may require **additional verification** from advertisers showing "potentially suspicious behavior" or running "categories likely to be targeted by scammers."
3. **Restricted goods and services** (allowed with conditions/permission — see §3): Alcohol; Commercial Exploitation of Crises; Dating; Hazardous Goods; Health and Wellness (weight loss, cosmetics, adult products/reproductive health); Historical Artifacts; Human Body Parts; Endangered/Non-Endangered Animals; Tobacco and Related Products; Weapons/Ammunition/Explosives; Drugs and Pharmaceuticals; Drug & Alcohol Addiction Treatment; Financial and Insurance Products and Services; Cryptocurrency Products and Services; Online Gambling and Games.
4. **Objectionable content**: Adult Nudity and Sexual Activity; Adult Sexual Exploitation; Adult Sexual Solicitation and Sexually Explicit Language; Bullying and Harassment; Profanity; Privacy Violations and **Personal Attributes**; Violent and Graphic Content; Suicide/Self-Injury/Eating Disorders.
5. **Intellectual property infringement**: Third-Party IP Infringement (incl. counterfeit goods); Using Meta Intellectual Property and Licenses.
6. **Social issue, electoral or political advertising** (see §4).
7. **Product and format-specific policies**: Video Ads (no disruptive tactics like flashing screens; entertainment trailers need prior written permission + 18+ targeting); **Lead Ads** (13 restricted question types — see below); Targeting (no discriminatory/predatory use); Relevance (ad must match landing page); Branded Content (must tag partner with the branded content tool).

Practitioner distinction "**prohibited vs restricted content**" maps here: groups 1–2 + most of 4 = outright prohibition; group 3 = "restricted content" set, ads can run with conditions, age gating, or written permission.

Four stated policy principles (official): protect from unsafe/discriminatory practices; protect from fraud/scams; promote positive user experiences (basis of "negative self-perception" and sensational-content rules); promote transparency (Ad Library; political ads stored 7 years).

### Lead Ads restricted questions (format-specific, often missed)
Lead ad forms must not request, without prior written permission: account numbers; criminal history; financial info (bank/card numbers, credit score, income, net worth, debt); government IDs (SSN, passport, driver's license); health info; insurance info; political affiliation (never allowed, no permission route); race/ethnicity; religion; sexual orientation; trade-union membership; usernames/passwords. Also banned: recreating with custom questions what a Prefill Question already covers.

## 2. Personal attributes, sensational content, and "negative self-perception" — what they mean for ad copy

**Personal Attributes policy (official text):** "Ads must not contain content that asserts or implies personal attributes. This includes direct or indirect assertions or implications about a person's race, ethnicity, religion, beliefs, age, sexual orientation or practices, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health (including medical conditions), vulnerable financial status, voting status, membership in a trade union, criminal record, or name."

Practical interpretation for copy:
- Trigger: **"you" + sensitive attribute** — "Are you struggling with debt?", "Do you have diabetes?", "Are you overweight?", "Tired of dull, pigmented skin?". "You" alone is fine; paired with a sensitive trait it isn't (practitioner consensus, gripasmarketing.com, 2026-05).
- Fix: describe the **product**, not the person. "A care routine for thinning hair after 35" not "Are you 35 and balding?" (wittelsbach.ai, 2026-05).
- Negative framing can imply a sensitive attribute; positive framing alone doesn't fix it — describe product/use/routine without asserting the viewer's condition or promising unsubstantiated transformation.
- One of the most common, most confusing rejection reasons; Ads Manager label: "Personal Attributes." Distinguish from **Misleading/Unrealistic claims** (unverifiable promised outcome) and **Adult Content** (too much skin, even non-sexual).

**Negative self-perception (official, under Health and Wellness):** "Ads must not imply or attempt to generate negative self-perception or declare there is a perfect body type or appearance one should aspire to in order to promote diet, weight loss, cosmetic procedures or any other health-related products." Policy basis for the before/after crackdown.

**Before/after and transformation framing:** health/beauty/weight-loss/cosmetic content can violate negative-self-perception or misleading-claims rules via imagery, copy, testimonials, or landing page — moving the claim into a testimonial doesn't fix it. Remove problem-body emphasis, substantiate objective claims, review the full destination; appeal only after fixing the exact cited issue.

**Sensational / shocking content (official):** ads must not contain "shocking, sensational or excessively violent content"; video ads must not use "overly disruptive tactics, such as flashing screens."

**Misleading/unrealistic claims:** guarantees, miracle claims, deceptive price claims, unsupported outcomes → rejection + enforcement risk. Qualified wording doesn't cure an otherwise misleading claim. Substantiate objective claims; match landing page, disclosures, and offer to the ad.

**20% text-on-image rule:** retired as a hard rule. Text density can still affect readability/performance, but don't claim automatic rejection or delivery penalty without account evidence. Pre-2024 posts citing a hard 20% limit are outdated.

## 3. Restricted verticals: rules and permission routes (2025–2026)

### Alcohol
- Must comply with local laws, industry codes, licenses; include age + country targeting consistent with Meta's requirements and local law; **minimum targeting 18+**; banned outright in some countries by local law (official policy text).
- No written-permission form needed, but creative must not show excessive consumption and must comply with the video/entertainment restrictions.

### Online gambling and games
- Definition (official): "any product or service where anything of monetary value is included as part of a method of entry and prize." This sweeps in casinos, sportsbooks, poker, bingo, lotteries, **fantasy sports, sweepstakes casinos, skill-based/prize contests**.
- **Prior written permission required.** **July 2025:** approval process moved from email-based written permission to the **"Authorizations and verifications" tab in Meta Business Suite**; approvals now attach to **specific business portfolios and ad accounts**, not broadly to the advertiser (adamigo.ai, 2026-07).
- Each target market needs its **own license proof**; 18+ minimum targeting (or local legal age); strict geo-fencing.
- Affiliates/aggregators get no pass: a landing page referencing real-money play, bonuses, promo codes, or redirecting to an operator counts as a gambling ad — Meta crawls destination URLs and redirect paths.
- **2026-02-23:** social casino / free-to-play gambling-style ads banned in 19 markets (incl. India, Indonesia, Philippines, Thailand, Vietnam) (adamigo.ai).
- Context: Meta removed **159M+ scam ads in 2025, 92% before any user report** (adamigo.ai citing Meta data).

### Dating
- "Ads for dating services are only allowed with **prior written permission**" — apply via the form linked from the Advertising Standards dating policy page. Must follow Meta's dating targeting requirements and dating ad guidelines (official).

### Crypto / financial products
- **Cryptocurrency:** ads for trading platforms and software/services enabling monetisation, reselling, swapping or staking of crypto require **prior written permission** (application link on the standards page). Not every crypto ad needs it (e.g., educational content, hardware wallets, tax services historically exempt) — [uncertain: exact current exemption list, verify on the standards page before relying].
- **Financial and insurance products/services:** credit cards, loans, insurance ads must target **18+**, must not directly request PII/financial info in the ad. Advertisers may need to **verify business/individual identity and demonstrate authorization by the relevant regulator**; statutory disclosure requirements apply (official).
- **Drug & alcohol addiction treatment (US):** requires **LegitScript certification** + Meta permission application (official).

### Supplements / health claims
- Official rule: no ads promoting "illicit or recreational drugs, or other **unsafe substances, products or supplements, as determined by Meta at its sole discretion**." Prescription drugs, OTC medicines and cannabis-derived products have category-specific requirements.
- **Weight loss and dietary/health products: 18+ targeting mandatory. Cosmetic products/procedures/surgeries: 18+ targeting mandatory** (official Health and Wellness policy).
- No negative self-perception, no "perfect body" framing, no before/after (see §2). No unverifiable medical claims.
- Gotcha: a shared domain inherits risk — if your store sells compliant cosmetics plus non-compliant supplements on one domain, the whole domain's ads can be rejected ("landing page sells a restricted product") (wittelsbach.ai).

### Subscriptions
- No standalone "subscription services" category in the current standards; auto-renew/free-trial offers are policed under **Unacceptable Business Practices** and **Fraud/Scams/Deceptive Practices** — recurring-charge terms must be disclosed, and landing-page/ad mismatch (e.g., "free" claims hiding billing) triggers rejection and account risk. Regulators (FTC negative-option rule finalized Oct 2024) add legal exposure on top. [uncertain: Meta's exact subscription disclosure requirements — standards page fetched showed no dedicated subscription policy; treat FTC-style clear disclosure as the safe bar.]

### Adult content
- No nudity, sexually suggestive positions/activities, sexual solicitation, or sexually explicit language (official). Adult *products and reproductive health* fall under Health and Wellness — 18+ targeting, heavy creative constraints. Common false positive: too much skin in an innocent visual (swimwear, fitness) rejected as "Adult Content."

### Tobacco / weapons
- Tobacco, nicotine, e-cigarettes/vapes, paraphernalia: prohibited; only WHO-/FDA-approved cessation products may be advertised, with local-law compliance. Weapons, ammunition, explosives, weapon-modification accessories: prohibited (official).

## 4. Special Ad Categories (declaration flow + restrictions)

Four categories, **self-declared at campaign level** in Ads Manager (dropdown at top of campaign creation, "Special Ad Category"): **Financial Products and Services** (renamed/broadened from "Credit" in **Oct 2024** — now covers banking, insurance, loans, investments), **Employment**, **Housing**, **Social Issues, Elections, or Politics** (jonloomer.com; inbeat.co).

- **When required:** determined by the ad's actual content, the advertiser's country, and the targeted countries. Meta requires the matching category for campaigns related to housing, employment, financial products/services, or social issues/elections/politics; the restriction matrix differs by jurisdiction.
- **Targeting restrictions (Financial, Employment, Housing):** affected flows can restrict age, gender, postcode/radius, detailed targeting, lookalikes, saved audiences, custom-audience use, or instant-form questions; exact controls vary by category/jurisdiction. The **15-mile radius** and no-ZIP restrictions are documented for specific US anti-discrimination implementations — don't apply worldwide. Special Ad Audiences discontinued. Inspect the declared campaign and current official policy before stating which controls remain.
- **Social Issues/Elections/Politics:** requires identity **authorization** (advertiser must reside in the targeted country), a **"Paid for by" disclaimer** linked to a Page; ads stored in the Ad Library **7 years**. Applies to heavily debated social issues in any placement country — even non-political brands (healthcare, climate, immigration-adjacent messaging) get swept in.
- **2025-01-13 data restrictions:** domains associated with special-ad-category-implying data (health, financial) saw **Pixel/Conversions API data-sharing restrictions** — Meta restricted certain event sources for categorized advertisers (inbeat.co; verify current state in Events Manager, enforcement rolled out progressively).
- **Failure to declare** can cause rejection/enforcement. If classification is unclear, compare the offer against the current official category definition and ask Meta support when available — don't declare reflexively or route around the requirement.

## 5. The ad review process

Official mechanics (Transparency Center):
- Review is **primarily automated**, manual in some cases. Starts automatically before the ad runs; status shows **"In review."**
- **Typically completed within 24 hours**, sometimes longer. Practitioner data: most approvals in minutes to a few hours; manual/complex reviews 24–48h+ (getkoro.app, 2025-12).
- Reviewed: ad components (image, video, text, **audio**), **targeting**, and the **landing page/destination** incl. redirect paths.
- **Re-review at any time:** approved ads can be rejected later, including after going live. An edited ad is treated as a **new ad** and re-reviewed.
- Beyond ads, Meta reviews **business assets** — portfolio, ad accounts, Pages, user accounts — and restricts at any level. If a *user account* is restricted, other admins of the same ad account/business can still advertise.

## 6. Why ads get rejected — the recurring list

Combines policy categories with practitioner-observed rejection patterns. Vendor rejection/appeal rates aren't portable across markets, verticals, or review periods — diagnose from the exact policy code and affected destination.

Creative-level:
1. Before/after transformation imagery (health/beauty/fitness).
2. Zoomed-in "problem" body parts.
3. Text-heavy/unreadable creative — the 20% rule is retired; don't attribute rejection to text density without the displayed policy reason.
4. Translated/regional copy whose claim, nuance, or destination breaks compliance; no universal language-specific rejection multiplier established.
5. Negative emotional framing / personal-attribute phrasing.
6. Stacked currency symbols, excessive symbols/emoji ("low quality" flag).
7. Visuals resembling restricted categories (green pharma crosses, bottle silhouettes, casino motifs).

Copy-level:
8. "You" + sensitive attribute (Personal Attributes).
9. Guaranteed outcomes / unrealistic claims.
10. Unbacked medical claims ("cures," "doctor-approved").
11. Financial guarantees ("guaranteed returns," "risk-free").
12. Misleading discount math vs landing page.

Targeting/account-level:
13. Special Ad Category not declared.
14. Landing page mismatch (offer in ad not honored on page).
15. Landing page/domain sells restricted products elsewhere on the site.
16. Destination quality, redirect behavior, broken pages, or an offer that cannot be verified.
17. Account history — repeated or severe violations can contribute to broader review or restrictions (see §7).

Rejection email is generic; find the real reason in Ads Manager → hover the red **"Not delivering"** label → **"See details"** for the policy code (wittelsbach.ai).

## 7. Why ad accounts get restricted or disabled

Meta's documented restriction categories (Business Help Center, summarized by auditsocials.com, 2026-06):

| Category | What triggers it | First fix |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising Standards violation | Repeated/severe ad violations; restricted category run without permission; undeclared special ad category | Fix/remove offending ads, then appeal |
| Business or payment verification | A required verification is incomplete, or billing cannot be validated | Complete the requested verification, resolve billing |
| Unusual/suspicious activity | Login anomalies, possible compromise, sudden behavior change | Secure account, confirm identity, 2FA |
| Circumventing systems | Evading review or prior enforcement — cloaking or replacement assets used to bypass a restriction | Clean lane: stop the workaround, preserve evidence, correct the underlying issue, supported review route. Grey execution → `meta-grey-ops` |
| Low ad-account quality | Accumulated disapprovals, high negative feedback | Reduce disapproval rate over time |

Payment-specific triggers (graphed.com, 2025-12; photonpay.com, 2026-07):
- **Repeated failed payments** (expired card, insufficient funds, bank decline) → automatic suspension. Repeatedly swapping payment methods fast looks suspicious.
- A payment method can be rejected or need verification. Practitioner reports link some prepaid/virtual cards to more failures; Meta publishes no universal card-type ban.
- **Name mismatch** between card and account.
- Chargebacks can freeze billing and trigger review — last resort after investigating unauthorized charges via Meta's billing/support process; permanent ban possible but not a documented automatic outcome of every dispute.
- Meta may lower the billing threshold or daily spend limit after declined charges.
- Treat a failed transaction, payable balance, card verification/default status,
  payment-method eligibility, and account restriction as independent facts.
  `No payment due` does not erase a failed transaction or lift a restriction.
  Do not make an arbitrary top-up when no amount is due.

Behavioral/structural triggers:
- Sudden spend or login changes can coincide with risk review, but no official `>20%/day` enforcement threshold is published. Keep delivery-learning advice separate from account-integrity claims.
- Enforcement can affect connected people, accounts, Pages, portfolios, or other assets depending on the violation — don't claim an asset automatically "poisons" every newly connected account without evidence from the specific restriction.
- Customer-feedback and commerce-quality signals can affect delivery or selling privileges in applicable programs. Inspect the live account status; fixed score thresholds from practitioner sources aren't a universal policy rule.
- Review eligibility and time limits depend on the affected asset and enforcement reason — don't infer a universal reinstatement deadline from a secondary source.

**Circumventing Systems:** cloaking, obfuscated destinations, or replacement assets used to bypass a restriction — can trigger enforcement beyond the individual ad. Official: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/. Clean-lane remediation stays here; operational grey (filters, whites, replacement) → `meta-grey-ops`.

## 8. Appeals: Account Quality / Business Support Home flow

Where: **Business Support Home** (business.facebook.com/business-support-home) → **Account status overview** → select the restricted account/asset → see reason → **"What you can do"** section. Also reachable via facebook.com/accountquality. (bir.ch, 2025-08; auditsocials.com.)

For a rejected **ad**: Ads Manager → rejected ad → request review, or via Business Support Home → pick the account → pick the ad/ad set/campaign → **Request review**.

The four remediation paths Meta presents, depending on cause (bir.ch):
1. **Confirm your identity** — email/phone/ID/payment-method proof.
2. **Complete verification** — "Start verification" button in "What you can do."
3. **Enable two-factor authentication** — "Secure your account" in Account status overview.
4. **Request review** — the formal appeal.

Appeal mechanics and expectations:
- Official review guide: account-restriction reviews typically complete in 48 hours, can take longer — treat as expectation, not SLA. Request limits, eligibility, and appeal deadlines depend on the asset, reason, and notification; don't impose a universal 180-day cutoff from secondary guidance. (Official: https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/review-policy-guidelines)
- Fix the cited issue before requesting review; preserve the notification, policy code, asset ID, and submission date. Treat an in-product final-decision message as case-specific — don't infer it merely from a missing button.
- Effective appeal practice (practitioner): fix the underlying issue *before* appealing; address the specific policy code cited, under ~200 words; reference exact ad language; attach landing-page URL and a 1–2 line product description; don't resubmit the same flagged creative in parallel (wittelsbach.ai; gdtagency.stck.me).
- If the review button is absent, document the state and use only the support routes shown for that account. Support-channel availability varies; absence of one button doesn't itself prove a universal final-decision rule.
- In billing appeals, provide the ad-account ID, transaction/payment ID, current
  balance state, and only the last four digits of the old and replacement cards.
  Never include a token, app secret, full card number, CVV, or verification code.

## 9. Keeping an account healthy (preventive checklist)

- **Complete business verification when required:** use Security Center or the in-product verification prompt. Enter legal details matching accepted documents, follow displayed document/retry requirements, read the specific rejection reason. Paths, eligible documents, attempt limits vary by account.
- **Verify domains when required** for ownership, link editing, or a feature/policy flow — useful, but not a universal prerequisite for every ad destination.
- **Enable 2FA** for everyone with asset access — required portfolio-wide for certain portfolios, configurable elsewhere. Least privilege, real named users, no shared logins.
- **Stable, authorized payment method:** sufficient funds, consistent billing details. Resolve disputed/unauthorized charges through Meta's support/payment process; don't treat a legitimate dispute as an optimization tactic.
- **Controlled launch:** begin with budgets/volume the business can monitor, confirm billing and measurement, increase spend from observed economics. Don't present "warming" an account as an official trust-building requirement.
- **Asset due diligence:** confirm ownership, access, status, and unresolved restrictions before attaching an existing Page, data source, domain, or ad account. Don't assume every historical issue transfers to all connected assets.
- **Pre-flight creatives and destinations** against applicable policies. Repeated/severe violations can contribute to broader enforcement, but Meta publishes no universal strike-count threshold.
- **Watch feedback signals:** negative feedback (ad hides/reports) and post-purchase feedback score; pause ads accumulating negative feedback.
- Submit a concise, evidence-backed appeal through the exposed review path; avoid repeated duplicate submissions. Creating replacement accounts to evade an active restriction can compound enforcement risk.
