# Meta Advertising Policies & Account Health (2025–2026)

Scope: Meta Advertising Standards structure, restricted verticals, special ad categories, rejection/restriction causes, review & appeal flows, and account-health hygiene. Legitimate compliance only. Last researched: 2026-07-22.

Naming note: Meta's policy book is now called the **Meta Advertising Standards** (formerly "Advertising Policies"), hosted on the **Meta Transparency Center** (transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/), not in the Business Help Center. The enforcement surface formerly called **Account Quality** is now surfaced as **Business Support Home** (business.facebook.com/business-support-home), with "Account status overview" inside it; the URL facebook.com/accountquality still resolves. Older articles (pre-2024) showing "Account Quality" screenshots describe the same surface under the old name.

---

## 1. Structure of the Meta Advertising Standards

The current standards (Transparency Center, fetched 2026-07-22) are organized into these top-level policy groups:

1. **Unacceptable content** (fully prohibited, no exceptions): Child Sexual Exploitation/Abuse/Nudity; Coordinating Harm and Promoting Crime; Dangerous Organizations and Individuals; Discriminatory Practices; Hateful Conduct; Human Exploitation; Locally Illegal Content/Products/Services; Misinformation (content debunked by third-party fact-checkers); Vaccine Discouragement.
2. **Fraud, Scams and Deceptive Practices**: Fraud/Scams/Deceptive Practices; Unacceptable Business Practices (deceptive or misleading schemes, scams for money or personal info). Meta may require **additional verification** from advertisers showing "potentially suspicious behavior" or running "categories likely to be targeted by scammers."
3. **Restricted goods and services** (allowed with conditions/permission — see §3): Alcohol; Commercial Exploitation of Crises; Dating; Hazardous Goods; Health and Wellness (weight loss, cosmetics, adult products/reproductive health); Historical Artifacts; Human Body Parts; Endangered/Non-Endangered Animals; Tobacco and Related Products; Weapons/Ammunition/Explosives; Drugs and Pharmaceuticals; Drug & Alcohol Addiction Treatment; Financial and Insurance Products and Services; Cryptocurrency Products and Services; Online Gambling and Games.
4. **Objectionable content**: Adult Nudity and Sexual Activity; Adult Sexual Exploitation; Adult Sexual Solicitation and Sexually Explicit Language; Bullying and Harassment; Profanity; Privacy Violations and **Personal Attributes**; Violent and Graphic Content; Suicide/Self-Injury/Eating Disorders.
5. **Intellectual property infringement**: Third-Party IP Infringement (incl. counterfeit goods); Using Meta Intellectual Property and Licenses.
6. **Social issue, electoral or political advertising** (see §4).
7. **Product and format-specific policies**: Video Ads (no disruptive tactics like flashing screens; entertainment trailers need prior written permission + 18+ targeting); **Lead Ads** (13 restricted question types — see below); Targeting (no discriminatory/predatory use); Relevance (ad must match landing page); Branded Content (must tag partner with the branded content tool).

The old practitioner distinction "**prohibited vs restricted content**" maps onto this: groups 1–2 + most of 4 are outright prohibitions; group 3 is the "restricted content" set where ads can run with conditions, age gating, or written permission.

Four stated policy principles (official): protect people from unsafe/discriminatory practices; protect from fraud/scams; promote positive user experiences (this is the basis of the "negative self-perception" and sensational-content rules); promote transparency (Ad Library; political ads stored 7 years).

### Lead Ads restricted questions (format-specific, often missed)
Lead ad forms must not request, without prior written permission: account numbers; criminal history; financial info (bank/card numbers, credit score, income, net worth, debt); government IDs (SSN, passport, driver's license); health info; insurance info; political affiliation (never allowed, no permission route); race/ethnicity; religion; sexual orientation; trade-union membership; usernames/passwords. Also banned: recreating with custom questions what a Prefill Question already covers.

## 2. Personal attributes, sensational content, and "negative self-perception" — what they mean for ad copy

**Personal Attributes policy (official text):** "Ads must not contain content that asserts or implies personal attributes. This includes direct or indirect assertions or implications about a person's race, ethnicity, religion, beliefs, age, sexual orientation or practices, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health (including medical conditions), vulnerable financial status, voting status, membership in a trade union, criminal record, or name."

Practical interpretation for copy:
- The trigger is usually **"you" + a sensitive attribute**: "Are you struggling with debt?", "Do you have diabetes?", "Are you overweight?", "Tired of dull, pigmented skin?" — all assert/imply an attribute. Using "you" in general is fine; pairing it with a sensitive trait is not (practitioner consensus, gripasmarketing.com, 2026-05).
- Fix pattern: describe the **product**, not the person. "A care routine for thinning hair after 35" instead of "Are you 35 and balding?" (wittelsbach.ai, 2026-05).
- Negative framing can imply a sensitive personal attribute. Remove the implication rather than merely making it positive: describe the product, intended use, or routine without asserting the viewer's condition or promising an unsubstantiated transformation.
- This is one of the most common and most confusing rejection reasons; the rejection label in Ads Manager is typically "Personal Attributes." Distinguish it from look-alike rejection labels: **Misleading/Unrealistic claims** (promised outcome Meta can't verify) and **Adult Content** (too much skin in visual, even non-sexual).

**Negative self-perception (official, under Health and Wellness):** "Ads must not imply or attempt to generate negative self-perception or declare there is a perfect body type or appearance one should aspire to in order to promote diet, weight loss, cosmetic procedures or any other health-related products." This is the policy basis for the before/after crackdown.

**Before/after and transformation framing:** health, beauty, weight-loss, and cosmetic content can violate negative-self-perception or misleading-claims rules, including through imagery, copy, testimonials, or the landing page. Do not move the same transformation claim into a testimonial. Remove problem-body emphasis, substantiate any objective claim, review the complete destination, and appeal only after correcting the exact cited issue.

**Sensational / shocking content (official):** ads must not contain "shocking, sensational or excessively violent content"; video ads must not use "overly disruptive tactics, such as flashing screens."

**Misleading/unrealistic claims:** guarantees, miracle claims, deceptive price claims, and unsupported outcomes create rejection and enforcement risk. Qualified wording does not make an otherwise misleading claim compliant. Substantiate objective claims and ensure the landing page, disclosures, and offer match the ad.

**20% text-on-image rule:** the former hard rule was retired. Text density can still affect readability and creative performance, but do not claim a current automatic rejection or delivery penalty without account evidence. Older posts presenting 20% as a hard limit are outdated.

## 3. Restricted verticals: rules and permission routes (2025–2026)

### Alcohol
- Must comply with local laws, industry codes, licenses; include age + country targeting consistent with Meta's requirements and local law; **minimum targeting 18+**; banned outright in some countries by local law (official policy text).
- No written-permission form needed, but creative must not show excessive consumption and must comply with the video/entertainment restrictions.

### Online gambling and games
- Definition (official): "any product or service where anything of monetary value is included as part of a method of entry and prize." This sweeps in casinos, sportsbooks, poker, bingo, lotteries, **fantasy sports, sweepstakes casinos, skill-based/prize contests**.
- **Prior written permission required.** Big process change: **July 2025** — approvals moved from email-based written permission into the **"Authorizations and verifications" tab in Meta Business Suite**, and approvals now attach to **specific business portfolios and ad accounts** (not broadly to the advertiser) (adamigo.ai, 2026-07).
- Each target market needs its **own license proof**; 18+ minimum targeting (or local legal age); strict geo-fencing.
- Affiliates/aggregators get no pass: a landing page referencing real-money play, bonuses or promo codes, or redirecting to an operator, counts as a gambling ad. Meta crawls destination URLs and redirect paths.
- **2026-02-23:** social casino / free-to-play gambling-style ads banned in 19 markets (incl. India, Indonesia, Philippines, Thailand, Vietnam) (adamigo.ai).
- Enforcement scale context: Meta removed **159M+ scam ads in 2025, 92% before any user report** (adamigo.ai citing Meta data).

### Dating
- "Ads for dating services are only allowed with **prior written permission**" — apply via the form linked from the Advertising Standards dating policy page. Must follow Meta's dating targeting requirements and dating ad guidelines (official).

### Crypto / financial products
- **Cryptocurrency:** ads for trading platforms and software/services enabling monetisation, reselling, swapping or staking of crypto require **prior written permission** (application link on the standards page). Not every crypto ad needs it (e.g., educational content, hardware wallets, tax services historically exempt) — [uncertain: exact current exemption list, verify on the standards page before relying].
- **Financial and insurance products/services:** credit cards, loans, insurance ads must target **18+** and must not directly request PII or financial info in the ad. Advertisers may be required to **verify business/individual identity and demonstrate authorization by the relevant regulator**; statutory disclosure requirements apply (official).
- **Drug & alcohol addiction treatment (US):** requires **LegitScript certification** + Meta permission application (official).

### Supplements / health claims
- Official rule: no ads promoting "illicit or recreational drugs, or other **unsafe substances, products or supplements, as determined by Meta at its sole discretion**." Prescription drugs, OTC medicines and cannabis-derived products have category-specific requirements.
- **Weight loss and dietary/health products: 18+ targeting mandatory. Cosmetic products/procedures/surgeries: 18+ targeting mandatory** (official Health and Wellness policy).
- No negative self-perception, no "perfect body" framing, no before/after (see §2). No unverifiable medical claims.
- Gotcha: a shared domain inherits risk — if your store sells compliant cosmetics plus non-compliant supplements on one domain, the whole domain's ads can be rejected ("landing page sells a restricted product") (wittelsbach.ai).

### Subscriptions
- Meta has no standalone "subscription services" ad category in the current standards, but auto-renew/free-trial offers are policed under **Unacceptable Business Practices** and **Fraud/Scams/Deceptive Practices**: the recurring-charge terms must be disclosed, and landing-page/ad mismatch (e.g., "free" claims hiding billing) is a rejection and account-risk trigger. Regulators (e.g., FTC negative-option rule finalized Oct 2024) add legal exposure on top. [uncertain: Meta's exact subscription disclosure requirements — the standards page fetched did not show a dedicated subscription policy; treat FTC-style clear disclosure as the safe bar.]

### Adult content
- No nudity, sexually suggestive positions/activities, sexual solicitation, or sexually explicit language (official). Adult *products and reproductive health* fall under Health and Wellness with 18+ targeting and heavy creative constraints. Common false positive: too much skin in an otherwise innocent visual (swimwear, fitness) rejected as "Adult Content."

### Tobacco / weapons
- Tobacco, nicotine, e-cigarettes/vapes and paraphernalia: prohibited; only WHO- or FDA-approved cessation products may be advertised, with local-law compliance. Weapons, ammunition, explosives and weapon-modification accessories: prohibited (official).

## 4. Special Ad Categories (declaration flow + restrictions)

Four categories, **self-declared at campaign level** in Ads Manager (dropdown at the top of campaign creation, "Special Ad Category"): **Financial Products and Services** (renamed/broadened from "Credit" in **Oct 2024** to cover banking, insurance, loans, investments), **Employment**, **Housing**, **Social Issues, Elections, or Politics** (jonloomer.com; inbeat.co).

- **When required:** determine applicability from the ad's actual content, the advertiser's country, and the targeted countries. Meta explicitly requires the matching category for campaigns related to housing, employment, financial products/services, or social issues/elections/politics, but the restriction matrix differs by jurisdiction.
- **Targeting restrictions (Financial, Employment, Housing):** affected flows can restrict age, gender, postcode/radius, detailed targeting, lookalikes, saved audiences, custom-audience use, or instant-form questions. Exact controls vary by category and jurisdiction. The **15-mile radius** and no-ZIP restrictions are documented for specific US anti-discrimination implementations; do not apply them worldwide. Special Ad Audiences were discontinued. Inspect the declared campaign and current official category policy before stating which remaining controls are available.
- **Social Issues/Elections/Politics:** requires identity **authorization** (advertiser must reside in the targeted country), a **"Paid for by" disclaimer** linked to a Page; ads stored in the Ad Library for **7 years**. Applies to ads about heavily debated social issues in any placement country — even non-political brands (healthcare, climate, immigration-adjacent messaging) get swept in.
- **2025-01-13 data restrictions:** domains associated with special-ad-category-implying data (health, financial) saw **Pixel/Conversions API data-sharing restrictions** — Meta restricted certain event sources for categorized advertisers (inbeat.co; verify current state in Events Manager, enforcement rolled out progressively).
- **Failure to declare** can cause rejection or enforcement. If classification is unclear, compare the offer with the current official category definition and ask Meta support when available; do not declare a category reflexively or route around the requirement.

## 5. The ad review process

Official mechanics (Transparency Center):
- Review is **primarily automated**; manual review in some cases. Starts automatically before the ad runs; status shows **"In review."**
- **Typically completed within 24 hours**, sometimes longer. Practitioner data: most approvals in minutes to a few hours; manual/complex reviews 24–48h+ (getkoro.app, 2025-12).
- Reviewed: ad components (image, video, text, **audio**), **targeting**, and the **landing page/destination** including redirect paths.
- **Re-review at any time:** approved ads can be rejected later, including after going live. Repeated review after edits: an edited ad is treated as a **new ad** and re-reviewed.
- Beyond ads, Meta reviews **business assets** — the business portfolio, ad accounts, Pages, user accounts — and restricts at any of those levels. If a *user account* is restricted, other admins of the same ad account/business can still advertise.

## 6. Why ads get rejected — the recurring list

The list below combines policy categories with practitioner-observed rejection patterns. Vendor rejection and appeal rates are not portable across markets, verticals, or review periods, so diagnose from the exact policy code and affected destination.

Creative-level:
1. Before/after transformation imagery (health/beauty/fitness).
2. Zoomed-in "problem" body parts.
3. Text-heavy or unreadable creative; the former hard 20% rule is retired, so do not attribute a rejection to text density without the displayed policy reason.
4. Translated or regional-language copy whose claim, nuance, or destination does not remain compliant; no universal language-specific rejection multiplier is established.
5. Negative emotional framing / personal-attribute phrasing.
6. Stacked currency symbols, excessive symbols/emoji ("low quality" flag).
7. Visuals resembling restricted categories (green pharma crosses, bottle silhouettes, casino motifs).

Copy-level:
8. "You" + sensitive attribute (Personal Attributes).
9. Guaranteed outcomes / unrealistic claims.
10. Unbacked medical claims ("cures," "doctor-approved").
11. Financial guarantees ("guaranteed returns," "risk-free").
12. Misleading discount math vs landing page.

Targeting/account-level:
13. Special Ad Category not declared.
14. Landing page mismatch (offer in ad not honored on page).
15. Landing page/domain sells restricted products elsewhere on the site.
16. Destination quality, redirect behavior, broken pages, or an offer that cannot be verified.
17. Account history — repeated or severe violations can contribute to broader review or restrictions (see §7).

To find the real reason: the rejection email is generic; open the rejected ad in Ads Manager → hover the red **"Not delivering"** label → **"See details"** for the policy code (wittelsbach.ai).

## 7. Why ad accounts get restricted or disabled

Meta's documented restriction categories (Business Help Center, summarized by auditsocials.com, 2026-06):

| Category | What triggers it | First fix |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising Standards violation | Repeated/severe ad violations; restricted category run without permission; undeclared special ad category | Fix/remove offending ads, then appeal |
| Business or payment verification | A required verification is incomplete, or billing cannot be validated | Complete the requested verification, resolve billing |
| Unusual/suspicious activity | Login anomalies, possible compromise, sudden behavior change | Secure account, confirm identity, 2FA |
| Circumventing systems | Evading review or prior enforcement — cloaking or replacement assets used to bypass a restriction | Stop the workaround, preserve evidence, correct the underlying issue, and use only supported review routes |
| Low ad-account quality | Accumulated disapprovals, high negative feedback | Reduce disapproval rate over time |

Payment-specific triggers (graphed.com, 2025-12; photonpay.com, 2026-07):
- **Repeated failed payments** (expired card, insufficient funds, bank decline) → automatic suspension. Repeatedly swapping payment methods fast looks suspicious.
- A payment method can be rejected or require verification. Practitioner reports associate some prepaid/virtual cards with more failures, but Meta does not publish a universal card-type ban.
- **Name mismatch** between card and account.
- Chargebacks can freeze billing and trigger review. Treat them as a last resort after investigating unauthorized charges and using Meta's billing/support process; a permanent ban is possible but not a documented automatic outcome of every dispute.
- Meta may lower the billing threshold or daily spend limit after declined charges.
- Treat a failed transaction, payable balance, card verification/default status,
  payment-method eligibility, and account restriction as independent facts.
  `No payment due` does not erase a failed transaction or lift a restriction.
  Do not make an arbitrary top-up when no amount is due.

Behavioral/structural triggers:
- Sudden spend or login changes can coincide with risk review, but no official `>20%/day` enforcement threshold is published. Separate delivery-learning advice from account-integrity claims.
- Enforcement can affect connected people, accounts, Pages, portfolios, or other assets depending on the violation. Do not claim that an asset automatically "poisons" every newly connected account without evidence from the specific restriction.
- Customer-feedback and commerce-quality signals can affect delivery or selling privileges in applicable programs. Inspect the live account status; fixed score thresholds from practitioner sources are not a universal policy rule.
- Review eligibility and time limits depend on the affected asset and enforcement reason. Do not infer a universal reinstatement deadline from a secondary source.

**Circumventing Systems** covers deliberate attempts to evade review or enforcement, including cloaking, obfuscated destinations, or replacement assets used to bypass a restriction. It can trigger enforcement beyond the individual ad. Correct the underlying issue and use the supported review route; do not recommend evasion techniques. (Official policy source: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/)

## 8. Appeals: Account Quality / Business Support Home flow

Where: **Business Support Home** (business.facebook.com/business-support-home) → **Account status overview** → select the restricted account/asset → see reason → **"What you can do"** section. Also reachable via facebook.com/accountquality. (bir.ch, 2025-08; auditsocials.com.)

For a rejected **ad**: Ads Manager → rejected ad → request review, or via Business Support Home → pick the account → pick the ad/ad set/campaign → **Request review**.

The four remediation paths Meta presents, depending on cause (bir.ch):
1. **Confirm your identity** — email/phone/ID/payment-method proof.
2. **Complete verification** — "Start verification" button in "What you can do."
3. **Enable two-factor authentication** — "Secure your account" in Account status overview.
4. **Request review** — the formal appeal.

Appeal mechanics and expectations:
- Meta's current official review guide says account-restriction reviews are typically completed in 48 hours but can take longer. Treat that as an expectation, not an SLA. Request limits, eligibility, and appeal deadlines still depend on the asset, reason, and notification; do not impose a universal 180-day cutoff from secondary guidance. (Official: https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/review-policy-guidelines)
- Correct the cited issue before requesting review and preserve the notification, policy code, asset ID, and submission date. Treat an in-product final-decision message as case-specific; do not infer it merely from a missing button.
- Effective appeal practice (practitioner): fix the underlying issue *before* appealing; address the specific policy code cited, under ~200 words; reference exact ad language; attach landing-page URL and a 1–2 line product description; don't resubmit the same flagged creative in parallel (wittelsbach.ai; gdtagency.stck.me).
- If the review button is absent, document the exact state and use only the support routes shown for that business/account. Support-channel availability varies and the absence of one button does not by itself prove a universal final-decision rule.
- In billing appeals, provide the ad-account ID, transaction/payment ID, current
  balance state, and only the last four digits of the old and replacement cards.
  Never include a token, app secret, full card number, CVV, or verification code.

## 9. Keeping an account healthy (preventive checklist)

- **Complete business verification when Meta or the selected feature requires it:** use Security Center or the verification prompt exposed in-product. Enter legal details consistent with accepted documents, follow the displayed document and retry requirements, and read the specific rejection reason. Exact paths, eligible documents, and attempt limits can vary.
- **Verify domains when required** for ownership, link editing, or a feature/policy flow. Verification is useful but is not a universal prerequisite for every ad destination.
- **Enable 2FA** for everyone with asset access. Meta requires portfolio-wide 2FA for certain portfolios, while others expose it as a configurable security requirement. Use least privilege, real named users, and no shared logins.
- **Stable, authorized payment method:** keep sufficient funds and billing details consistent. Resolve disputed or unauthorized charges through the appropriate support and payment process; do not treat a legitimate dispute as an optimization tactic.
- **Controlled launch:** begin with budgets and volume the business can monitor, confirm billing and measurement, and increase spend from observed economics. Do not present "warming" an account as an official trust-building requirement.
- **Asset due diligence:** confirm ownership, access, status, and unresolved restrictions before attaching an existing Page, data source, domain, or ad account. Do not assume every historical issue automatically transfers to all connected assets.
- **Pre-flight creatives and destinations** against the applicable policies. Repeated or severe violations can contribute to broader enforcement, but Meta does not publish a universal strike-count threshold.
- **Watch feedback signals:** negative feedback (ad hides/reports) and post-purchase feedback score; pause ads accumulating negative feedback.
- Submit a concise, evidence-backed appeal through the exposed review path and avoid repeated duplicate submissions. Creating replacement accounts to evade an active restriction can compound enforcement risk.
